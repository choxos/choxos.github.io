About

# whoami

I trained as a dentist in Tehran, practiced as a community dentist in Kurdistan, and then followed the questions upstream: how do we know a treatment works, and can we trust the papers that say so? That took me to an MSc in Health Research Methodology at McMaster and into evidence synthesis for good.

Today I run pairwise and network meta-analyses and population-adjusted indirect treatment comparisons (MAIC, STC, ML-NMR) as a Research Associate in Comparative Effectiveness at Cytel. Outside the day job I turn methods into software: CRAN packages, open textbooks, and browser tools that anyone can rerun and audit.

I have used GNU/Linux since 2010, translated A Byte of Python into Persian in 2009, and have served Cochrane for most of a decade, from Cochrane Iran to the Cochrane Council.

## current focus

  • Population-adjusted indirect treatment comparisons and disconnected evidence networks
  • Target trial emulation and causal inference for real-world evidence
  • Research integrity tooling: retractions, citations, DOIs, and transparency indicators
  • Interactive teaching tools for anatomy, mathematics, and statistics

## research software should be

  1. Reproducible enough for another analyst to rerun it.
  2. Transparent enough for a reviewer to audit it.
  3. Documented enough for a tired user to understand it.
  4. Small and boring where possible; cleverness is not a substitute for reliability.

Open science over closed claims.
Reproducible code over screenshots.
Useful tools over vanity metrics.
